Etsy Offsite Ads Fees: 12%, 15%, and the $100 Cap

An Offsite Ads fee belongs to an attributed order, not every Etsy sale. Confirm the rate Etsy assigns to your shop, then include the fee as its own line before judging the order's profit.

Etsy's Offsite Ads fee is 15% for an attributed order in the standard participating tier and 12% for the reduced-rate tier Etsy assigns after the shop passes its sales threshold. The fee is capped at $100 for one order. The cap limits this fee line only; transaction, payment-processing, listing, and other applicable charges still remain.

1. The short answer: first confirm that the order was attributed

Offsite Ads can place Etsy listings on external advertising channels. Etsy's policy says a fee can apply when a buyer clicks an Offsite Ad and then purchases from the shop within 30 days. The purchased item does not have to be the same listing that appeared in the ad.

Do not apply 12% or 15% to every order as an assumed marketing average. For an existing sale, use the attribution and fee shown by Etsy. For a future-price scenario, compare a non-attributed order with the applicable attributed-order rate so the exposure is visible.

Keep Etsy Ads separate. Etsy Ads is the on-site advertising budget a seller chooses. Offsite Ads is an attributed-order fee under a different program. Do not enter the same charge as both allocated Etsy Ads spend and an Offsite Ads fee.

2. Why one shop can see 15% and another 12%

Etsy uses a shop's sales over a rolling 365-day period to determine Offsite Ads participation and the applicable rate. The current guidance describes 15% for participating shops that have always made less than $10,000 in any consecutive 365-day period and a reduced 12% rate after a shop has made $10,000 or more in one of those periods.

A single order amount cannot reveal the shop's tier. Check the Offsite Ads section in Shop Manager or the actual statement rather than inferring 12% from a large order or 15% from a small one.

3. Attribution is an order fact, not a pricing guess

Etsy's Advertising & Marketing Policy describes a 30-day attribution window after the buyer clicks an Offsite Ad. If the buyer later places an order from that shop during the window, the order can be attributed even when it contains a different listing. Etsy also says multiple qualifying clicks can still result in one Offsite Ads fee on an order.

MarginGauge does not decide whether Etsy's attribution is correct and does not recreate click history. It asks for a scenario: no attribution, Offsite Ads at 15%, or Offsite Ads at 12%. Use Etsy's records for a completed order and use explicit sensitivity cases for pricing.

4. What amount the percentage uses

For the supported US model, the Offsite Ads base is seller order revenue: merchandise after a seller-funded item discount, plus buyer-paid personalization, shipping, and gift wrap. Marketplace sales tax collected from the buyer is excluded from this advertising fee base for US orders.

Offsite Ads fee = min(round-cent(applicable rate x seller order revenue), $100.00)

An Etsy-funded coupon does not reduce the seller's revenue or ordinary fee bases in the current official guidance. A seller-funded discount does reduce merchandise revenue before the Offsite Ads percentage is applied. Sales tax can still increase the separate payment-processing fee even though it does not enter the Offsite Ads base.

5. A $130 order at 15%, 12%, and no attribution

Assume one item priced at $120.00 and $10.00 buyer-paid shipping. Product cost is $45.00, packaging is $5.00, and the actual shipping label is $10.00. There is no sales tax, discount, personalization, gift wrap, Texas seller-fee tax, or allocated Etsy Ads spend.

The seller order revenue is $130.00. Before Offsite Ads, the engine calculates an $8.45 transaction fee, $4.15 payment-processing fee, and $0.20 listing allocation, or $12.80 in Etsy fees.

6. The $100 cap applies per order

At $1,000.00 of seller order revenue, 15% would be $150 and 12% would be $120 before the limit. The existing engine returns $100.00 for the 15% case and $100.00 for the 12% case.

The cap does not make the rest of the order fee-free. On this $1,000 no-tax, one-item example, the standard transaction, payment-processing, and listing lines total $95.45. Adding the capped Offsite Ads line produces $195.45 in total modeled Etsy fees before seller operating costs.

Do not split an order to model the cap. The official rule is a maximum Offsite Ads fee for a single order. MarginGauge applies the cap to the order being calculated and does not infer separate buyer checkouts or later adjustments.

7. Model the attributed and non-attributed outcomes

Open the Etsy Profit Calculator and enter the complete order first. Keep shipping charged separate from the actual label cost. Then run the order with no attribution and with the applicable Offsite Ads tier.

  • Use the 12% scenario only after confirming Etsy assigned the reduced-rate tier.
  • Use the 15% scenario for a participating shop when Etsy shows that rate.
  • Use no attribution when the order was not attributed or when testing the ordinary case.
  • Include seller-funded discounts before comparing the fee; do not subtract marketplace sales tax from seller revenue.
  • Keep Etsy Ads spend, product costs, labor, packaging, and actual shipping in their own cost fields.

For the full fee map, read Etsy Fees for US Sellers. To understand contribution rather than payout, read How to Calculate Etsy Profit Per Order. To build the fee into a future price, use How to Price Etsy Products for Profit.
