Does Etsy Charge Fees on Shipping? A Profit Example

Etsy treats buyer-paid shipping as part of the seller order amount used for key fees. Your carrier label is a separate cost. Keeping those two numbers apart shows whether a shipping charge actually funds fulfillment.

Yes. For a supported US Etsy Marketplace order, buyer-paid shipping is included in the seller order revenue used for the 6.5% transaction fee. It is also included in the Etsy Payments processing gross under the cited US processing guidance. The shipping label you purchase is not that revenue; it is a separate seller cost.

1. The short answer: shipping is not automatically free of fees

Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy describes the transaction fee as 6.5% of the price displayed for a listing plus the amount charged for shipping and gift wrapping. The same policy says payment processing is assessed on the sale amount and applicable tax and shipping. For the supported calculator model, buyer-paid shipping therefore increases the fee bases instead of sitting outside the order.

That does not mean Etsy keeps the shipping charge as a separate commission. It means the charge is part of the order amount on which percentage fees are calculated. The amount you pay to USPS, UPS, or another carrier is not inferred from the buyer charge and must be entered as an actual cost.

Keep the labels precise. “Shipping charged” is what the buyer pays you on the Etsy receipt. “Actual shipping” is what you pay for the label or carrier service. A free-shipping listing sets the first number to zero; it does not set the second number to zero.

2. Put buyer-paid shipping and actual shipping in different fields

Start with the amount that belongs to the seller order. In MarginGauge's notation, R is seller gross order revenue before Etsy fees and excluding marketplace sales tax:

R = discounted merchandise + personalization + shipping charged + gift wrap charged

Then list the costs required to fulfill the order. Product cost, packaging, labor, insurance, and the actual shipping label reduce contribution profit. They do not change the buyer-paid shipping amount in the fee base.

InputWhat it meansWhere it belongs

Etsy shipping-label prices vary with carrier, service, origin, destination, weight, and package dimensions. Use the amount for the package you will actually ship. Do not copy a generic shipping estimate into both fields.

3. A complete order with a $6 shipping charge

This example uses one physical item and no sales tax, discounts, ads, attribution, or Texas seller-fee tax. The item sells for $30.00. The buyer pays $6.00 for shipping. Your product cost is $10.00, packaging is $1.00, and the actual label costs $5.00.

Enter $30.00 as the unit listing price, $6.00 as shipping charged, and $5.00 as fulfillment shipping. Enter $10.00 COGS and $1.00 packaging. The calculator keeps the $6.00 buyer charge in R and the $5.00 label in operating costs.

The percentage fee bases use the $36.00 seller order revenue. The $16.00 cost total is $10.00 product cost + $1.00 packaging + $5.00 actual shipping. The result is $16.13 contribution profit, before any fixed business costs or income taxes.

4. Compare the same order with free shipping

Now keep the product, packaging, and actual $5.00 label unchanged, but set shipping charged to $0.00. The seller order revenue becomes $30.00. The transaction fee is $1.95, processing is $1.15, and the allocated listing fee remains $0.20. Total Etsy fees are $3.30 and contribution profit is $10.70.

Charging $6.00 for a $5.00 label adds $6.00 of seller revenue and $0.57 of additional Etsy fees in this example, leaving $5.43 more contribution. The result is not a universal shipping markup recommendation: compare it with your actual label, packaging, conversion rate, and competitive price.

5. Model your own shipping decision

Open the Etsy Profit Calculator and enter the buyer charge and actual fulfillment amount separately. Begin with Unit economics when you want to know whether the order contributes profit over repeated sales. Use Payment Account estimate only when you are modeling order-related Etsy account activity and know which costs were billed through Etsy.

  • Use the actual net shipping charge after a shipping promotion, not the amount you hoped to collect.
  • Use a label quote or receipt for the package's actual weight, dimensions, route, and service.
  • Keep insurance separate when it is not already included in the label cost.
  • Include sales tax from the receipt when you want to estimate processing; MarginGauge treats it as pass-through money.
  • Test Offsite Ads, Share & Save, discounts, and Texas seller-fee tax as separate supported scenarios rather than adding a generic percentage.

For the broader fee bases, read Etsy Fees for US Sellers. For the full order model, read How to Calculate Etsy Profit Per Order. If you are choosing a price that should survive a shipping promotion, read How to Price Etsy Products for Profit.
