How to Price Etsy Products for Profit

Start with the cost of fulfilling an order, choose how much contribution you need, and test the selling price against Etsy fees. A price that covers materials alone can still leave too little for your time and your business.

Your target price is the amount that leaves the contribution you want after the variable costs of the modeled order. It is a financial threshold, not evidence that buyers will accept that price.

Use the Etsy Profit Calculator and select Target price to work through this guide. Keep the input assumptions visible: changing quantity, shipping, promotions, or attribution can change the answer.

1. Build a cost floor that includes your time

Collect the costs before setting a target. For a handmade physical item, record materials or purchased product cost per unit as COGS. Record labor separately if it is not already included in that figure. Counting the same labor in both fields overstates costs; omitting it can make a price look sustainable when it is not.

  • Product cost: materials or acquisition cost for each sold unit, using your own records.
  • Variable labor: the order's production and fulfillment time valued at your chosen hourly rate. This is your assumption, not an Etsy wage rule.
  • Packaging: the box, mailer, padding, and other consumables used for this order.
  • Actual shipping: the label or carrier cost you pay. Keep insurance separate when it is not already included in the label amount.
  • Other variable costs and Etsy Ads: include relevant amounts once; an Etsy Ads allocation is your estimate, not an automatic order attribution.

Shipping charged to the buyer is revenue. Actual shipping paid to the carrier is a cost. Offering free shipping sets the first amount to zero while the second still needs funding. Etsy's fees policy says label prices depend on carrier, origin, destination, weight, and dimensions, so use an actual quote for the package you plan to ship.

Fixed rent, subscriptions, equipment, income tax, and unsold inventory are outside this order contribution model. The contribution you retain still has to help fund the wider business. Review those obligations separately before deciding that the product is commercially viable.

2. Choose a dollar profit target or a contribution margin

A target contribution profit is the dollar amount you want left from the whole simulated order after Etsy marketplace fees and all entered variable costs. It is separate from labor compensation: in the example below, labor is paid for within costs before the $10 target is met.

Contribution margin is contribution profit divided by seller order revenue before Etsy fees and excluding marketplace sales tax. A 40% margin target means at least 40% of that revenue remains as contribution. Adding 40% to materials cost is a markup and does not establish a 40% contribution margin.

Choose one target at a time. There is no universal Etsy-approved margin in this guide. Use a target that fits your costs and business needs. For the underlying profit definitions, see how to calculate Etsy profit per order.

3. Price one item for a $10 contribution

This hypothetical order contains one physical item. Product cost is $5.00. Labor is $3.00, representing ten minutes at a seller-chosen $18 hourly rate. Packaging costs $1.00 and the actual shipping label costs $4.00. These four costs total $13.00.

Set quantity to 1, shipping charged to $0, and personalization and gift wrap to $0. Select no discount, no attribution, and the explicit No tax scenario. This example excludes Texas seller-fee tax and all other manual costs, insurance, and Etsy Ads allocations. No tax is a scenario assumption, not a claim that the real buyer owes no tax.

In Target price, enter a profit target of $10.00 and calculate. The existing MarginGauge calculation engine returns a minimum pre-discount unit listing price of $25.91.

The buyer pays $25.91 under these assumptions. After $2.91 in Etsy fees, $23.00 remains. The $13.00 of product and fulfillment costs leaves $10.00 contribution. The same engine gives only $9.99 contribution at $25.90, so that adjacent price misses the target.

The example was generated and checked with the calculator's existing Target Price and Order Profit engine, using catalog etsy-us-2026-09-03.1 and calculation contract 1.0.0. Each fee line uses MarginGauge's half-up cent rounding; the $0.20 per sold unit is its Unit economics allocation. These are disclosed estimation conventions, not a guarantee that an Etsy statement will match every cent.

4. Stress-test the price before publishing a listing

Sales and percentage discounts

A seller-funded promotion lowers what you retain from merchandise. In Target price, enter the intended discount before calculating: the result is the listing price before that discount. MarginGauge applies a percentage merchandise discount once to the merchandise subtotal. Do not apply that percentage a second time to shipping, personalization, or gift wrap in this model.

Test your regular price and promotion as separate scenarios. A price that meets the target without a discount does not automatically meet it during a sale. Etsy-funded coupons and stacked seller/Etsy-funded discounts are outside Target Price support; do not treat the two funding sources as interchangeable.

Sales tax and fees

The official US transaction fee is 6.5% and excludes sales tax. Payment processing for a US bank account is 3% plus $0.25 per order, with applicable tax and shipping in its base. The US Etsy fee guide explains the separate bases.

For a tax sensitivity check, choose a custom effective rate on seller revenue. This is a user-defined scenario, not an address-based tax engine or a standard US tax rate. Recalculate rather than assuming tax has no effect on your price: tax is pass-through money, but it can increase processing costs.

Offsite Ads exposure

Etsy's current fees policy sets an Offsite Ads fee of 15% or 12% for qualifying attributed orders, capped at $100 per order. Confirm your shop's applicable tier before choosing the scenario. Compare the target price with and without the applicable fee. The Offsite Ads guide explains the attribution window, fee base, tiers, and cap. This comparison does not let you change how Etsy attributed an existing order.

Do not add a generic percentage buffer and call it exact. Separate fee lines, fixed fees, caps, and cent rounding all matter. Let the same calculator evaluate each supported scenario using its current rules.

5. Turn the threshold into a selling decision

The $25.91 example is a minimum for one set of inputs. You may choose a higher asking price to leave more contribution, but assess demand, comparable products, positioning, and the value buyers receive. If the market will not support the required price, revisit production time, materials, package design, or the product itself rather than hiding a cost.

Target Price supports one listing with a fixed quantity. If quantity is greater than one, the dollar target belongs to the whole order; COGS scales per unit, while entered labor and fulfillment amounts should describe that whole order. Mixed baskets belong in Order profit. A missing reliable rule or a target with no solution under the tool's $1,000,000-per-unit guardrail produces an error, not a suggested approximate price. That cap is a MarginGauge limit, not an Etsy listing limit.
